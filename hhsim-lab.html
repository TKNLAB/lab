Gates along the axon

How to read this view

A sample of the channels in the patch. Each gate opens at random with probability m, h or n; a channel conducts only when all its gates are open.

  • Na⁺ channel: three m gates (dots above) and one h gate
  • K⁺ channel: four n gates
  • Dot filled = activation gate open, hollow = closed
  • h gate: hangs below when open, plugs the pore when closed
  • Coloured centre = channel conducting
  • Blocked by TTX or TEA; faded = channel switched off
  • ↓Arrows at right: size and direction of INa and IK (down = inward)

m³h and n⁴ are the exact open fractions; the drawing is a random sample of them.

Each bar is one membrane current at the cursor, pointer or now, on a log scale. Left = inward (depolarising), right = outward. I ionic is their sum. In current clamp C·dV/dt = I injected − I ionic, so V rises whenever the inward side wins.

V plotted against n. The trajectory shows the path of the membrane state over time. Red dots: V-nullcline (dV/dt = 0); blue curve: n-nullcline (dn/dt = 0). Where they cross is the rest point. A spike is one large loop; repetitive firing settles into a closed loop, the limit cycle.

An independent web re-implementation inspired by HHsim (Touretzky et al., Carnegie Mellon University, GPL), extended with an axon model, analysis tools and guided lessons. Model: Hodgkin & Huxley (1952) squid axon kinetics referenced to 6.3 °C, Nernst potentials from ion concentrations, passive Na⁺/K⁺/Cl⁻ leak, Q10-scaled gating, an ideal voltage clamp, a 1-D cable for the axon, and Fox–Lu Langevin channel noise. Units: mV, ms, nF, µS, nA, mM. Integration: exponential Euler (Δt = 5 µs patch, 10 µs axon with implicit axial coupling).

How to use HHsim

Three ways to record

Current clamp injects current and records voltage, like a sharp electrode. Voltage clamp holds the voltage and records the currents, like Hodgkin and Huxley's experiments. Axon joins thousands of patches into a cable so you can watch a spike travel.

Start here

Press Stim 1. The simulation runs until the membrane settles, then stops. Run keeps it going; Nudge advances a little. Clear wipes the traces but keeps your parameters. Recall returns to rest (or to a state you stored with Store).

Reading the screen

Hover over any trace to read values; click to place a cursor. The lower-right panel shows the channels' gates, a live balance of currents, or the phase plane, all synchronised with the pointer or cursor. Measure marks threshold, peak, half-width and the after-hyperpolarisation. Pin trace keeps a response as a dashed reference so you can compare before and after a change.

Learning

The Lessons column (button in the top bar) has guided experiments that set themselves up, and challenges that check your answers against the model. The Analysis tab measures whole relationships: threshold, strength–duration, f–I and refractory curves, and conduction velocity against diameter.

About the model

Rate constants are Hodgkin and Huxley's 1952 squid-axon fits. Concentrations set the Nernst potentials. Conductances are for a patch with 1 nF capacitance, so 1 µS corresponds to 1 mS/cm². The M-current and persistent Na⁺ presets, myelination and channel noise are teaching extensions, not part of the original model.
