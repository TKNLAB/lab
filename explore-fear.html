A fear memory, written with light

In 2014 Nabavi and colleagues put a light-gated channel into the auditory inputs to the rat lateral amygdala. Light paired with a mild footshock made light alone frightening. The paired synapse had potentiated. Optical LTD at that synapse erased the memory, and optical LTP brought it back. This page runs that experiment on a model synapse, with the controls that make the result mean something.

02 · The plasticity rule

Calcium decides: a little depresses, a lot potentiates

Every protocol raises calcium in the synapse. While calcium is above a lower threshold, the synapse is pushed towards depression; above a higher threshold, towards potentiation. What the protocol does depends on how long calcium spends in each band. Slow light pulses stay in the lower band. Fast trains, or light during a shock, reach the upper one.

The detail · the synapse model+

Each synapse carries a variable ρ between 0 and 1 with two stable states, DOWN and UP; its strength is w0 in DOWN and w1 = 5·w0 in UP. Calcium c(t) drives it:

τ dρ/dt = −ρ(1 − ρ)(ρ* − ρ) + γp(1 − ρ)Θ[c − θp] − γdρΘ[c − θd] + noise
θd = 1, θp = 1.3, γd = 200, γp = 321.8, τ = 150 s, σ = 2.83, ρ* = 0.5, τCa = 20 ms

These are Graupner & Brunel's published values. During a protocol the potentiation and depression terms dominate, so, as in their own analysis, ρ is treated as an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process whose drift and noise switch with the calcium band. Calcium decays exponentially between events, so the time spent in each band is computed exactly, and the probability that a DOWN synapse ends UP (or an UP synapse ends DOWN) follows from the resulting Gaussian without random sampling. The fraction of synapses in UP, β, then gives the mean strength 1 + 4β (in units of w0).

The calcium each light pulse produces is ours. Release depresses with use (U = 0.71, recovery 2.7 s), so slow pulses each release fully and fast trains release less per pulse. An NMDA term grows with postsynaptic depolarisation, which builds up during a fast train and is strong during the shock; the shock also adds back-propagating spikes at 40 Hz. MK-801 removes 90% of the NMDA calcium. These eight numbers were fitted together so that the published field-EPSP changes and the conditioning AMPA/NMDA change are reproduced; naive synapses start with 13% UP.

Context

From a potentiated synapse to an engram

1997Two groups showed that fear conditioning itself potentiates synapses in the amygdala: in vivo responses to the auditory cue grow after pairing, and synaptic currents in slices from conditioned animals are larger. [5] [6]
2010Optical activation of lateral amygdala pyramidal cells could stand in for the shock and instruct fear learning to a tone. [7]
2012–13Hippocampal cells active during learning were tagged with ChR2. Reactivating them with light later produced freezing, and pairing them with a shock in a different context created a false memory. [8] [9]
2014Nabavi and colleagues closed the loop at a single input: memory written by LTP, removed by LTD and restored by LTP, all with light. [1]
Methods note

What this model is, and what it is not

The plasticity rule and its parameters are published (Graupner & Brunel 2012). The protocols are those of Nabavi et al. 2014. The calcium each protocol produces, the naive fraction of potentiated synapses and the behavioural read-out are ours, fitted so that the model reproduces the paper's field-EPSP and AMPA/NMDA numbers; the fitted values are listed in section 02.

The circuit is reduced. Individual neurons are not simulated: the lateral amygdala's response to the optical cue is taken as proportional to the mean strength of the light-driven synapses, and the central amygdala output as a steep sigmoid of that response, with its threshold placed between the strength after LTD and after LTP. Extinction is modelled as inhibition added downstream of the lateral amygdala, which grows with each unreinforced cue that still evokes a response (after Pape & Paré 2010). Freezing is drawn as a smaller response moving with lever-press suppression; the paper reports the two correlate with R² = 0.4.

These are simulations of a published plasticity model, for explanation. Experimental data appear in the papers cited below.
