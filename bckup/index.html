Why ion channels?

Ion channels generate the electrical signals of the nervous system and are central to our perception of who we are. When mutations change how they work, the consequences can be severe. More than 60 human channelopathies have been described, including epilepsy, cystic fibrosis, congenital myasthenic syndromes, lethal multiple pterygium syndrome and cardiac arrhythmias. Not surprisingly, nearly a fifth of all drugs act on ion channels.

What we do

The Ion Channel and Receptor Biology Lab studies the structure and function of ion channels and synaptic receptors, and their roles in human disease. We combine single-channel and whole-cell patch-clamp electrophysiology, stochastic data analysis and kinetic modelling with protein engineering, molecular dynamics simulations, realistic neuronal simulations, microscopy and transcriptomics. We work in mammalian cells and primary chick embryo neurons.
